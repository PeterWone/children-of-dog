# **I. The Almanac at the Level of the Individual**
### **1. Apprentices**
For apprentices, the Almanac is:

- a rite of passage  
- a training manual  
- a map of the civilisation  
- a record of mistakes and corrections  
- a promise that knowledge will not be lost  

They learn:

- how to read diagnostic trees  
- how to interpret soil charts  
- how to follow seasonal cycles  
- how to maintain tools  
- how to cross‑reference villages’ specialisations  

Copying sections of the Almanac is a **coming‑of‑age ritual**.  
It teaches humility, precision, and the idea that knowledge is communal.

### **2. Masters**
For masters, the Almanac is:

- a reference  
- a ledger of obligations  
- a record of what has been promised  
- a tool for coordinating with other villages  

Masters contribute:

- field notes  
- corrections  
- new heuristics  
- updated diagrams  
- improved processes  

Their authority comes not from rank, but from **contributions to the Almanac**.

### **3. Elders**
For elders, the Almanac is:

- a memory prosthetic  
- a cultural stabiliser  
- a way to ensure continuity  
- a check against their own biases  

Elders use it to:

- schedule inspections  
- assign apprentices  
- coordinate inter‑village projects  
- maintain ecological balance  

The Almanac keeps elders honest.

# **II. The Almanac at the Level of the Village**
### **1. Governance Without Government**
The Almanac replaces:

- councils  
- bureaucracies  
- ministries  
- regulatory bodies  

Villages don’t vote.  
They **consult the Almanac**.

It contains:

- water‑rights agreements  
- charcoal quotas  
- rail‑usage schedules  
- ecological thresholds  
- maintenance cycles  
- apprenticeship rotations  

No one enforces it.  
Everyone respects it.

### **2. Local Adaptation**
Each village maintains:

- a local supplement  
- seasonal notes  
- soil‑specific data  
- pest‑pressure logs  
- rainfall deviations  
- tool‑failure reports  

These feed into the next convocation.

### **3. Inter‑Village Coordination**
Villages use the Almanac to:

- plan rail shipments  
- coordinate charcoal production  
- schedule kiln firings  
- manage ore distribution  
- time harvests  
- allocate apprentices  

It is the **mesh’s shared calendar**.

# **III. The Almanac at the Level of the Mesh (Civilisation‑Wide)**
### **1. The Convocation**
Every season, specialists gather to:

- compare notes  
- resolve discrepancies  
- update terminology  
- standardise measurements  
- cross‑validate ecological data  
- share innovations  

This is where the Almanac is **reborn**.

### **2. The Printers**
The printers are:

- editors  
- archivists  
- cross‑linkers  
- standardisers  
- cultural stewards  

They:

- distill conversations  
- remove contradictions  
- merge parallel discoveries  
- ensure clarity  
- maintain continuity  

They are the civilisation’s **anti‑entropy mechanism**.

### **3. The Seasonal Release**
Each edition includes:

- stable knowledge (unchanged)  
- updated knowledge (refined)  
- diagnostic trees (modular)  
- seed catalogues (localised)  
- rail schedules (dynamic)  
- ecological maps (critical)  
- inspection assignments (binding)  

The Almanac is not a book.  
It is a **snapshot of the civilisation’s current best understanding**.

### **1. Transparency**
Everything is:

- public  
- documented  
- cross‑checked  
- peer‑reviewed  
- open to apprentices  

No one can hide:

- inflated requests  
- false claims  
- resource hoarding  
- ecological damage  

The Almanac makes deception **structurally impossible**.

### **2. Constraint Network**
The Almanac encodes:

- ecological limits  
- material limits  
- labour limits  
- rail capacity  
- charcoal cycles  

If someone tries to cheat, the constraints break.  
Everyone sees it.

### **3. Cultural Immunity**
Teenagers copy the Almanac.  
They internalise:

- the logic  
- the constraints  
- the expectations  
- the humility  

This prevents elite drift.
