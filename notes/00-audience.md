## 🎯 Primary Audience: Readers Who Love “Competence in Crisis”
These readers gravitate toward stories where:
- A highly capable but non‑superheroic protagonist solves problems through skill, experience, and grit  
- Systems thinking, logistics, and practical knowledge matter  
- Communities respond realistically to disaster  
- The tone is grounded, wry, and adult  

This is the same audience that devours:
- *The Martian* (Weir)  
- *Project Hail Mary* (Weir)  
- *Seveneves* (Stephenson)  
- *The Last of Us* (for the grounded survival + community dynamics)  
- *Station Eleven* (Mandel)  
- *The Expanse* (Corey) — especially the Belter/OPA arcs  
- *Old Man’s War* (Scalzi) — for the voice and humour  

They want:
- Realistic problem‑solving  
- High‑stakes survival  
- A protagonist who is smart, stubborn, and funny  
- A world that feels lived‑in and technically plausible  
- A tone that respects their intelligence  

Your novel fits this perfectly.

## 🔧 Secondary Audience: “Engineering Fiction” Readers
This is a surprisingly large niche: people who love stories where engineering, logistics, and practical know‑how are central to the plot.

They read:
- Andy Weir  
- Neal Stephenson  
- Craig Alanson (the *Mavericks* books especially)  
- John Ringo’s *Black Tide Rising* (minus the politics)  
- Larry Niven’s *The Integral Trees* and *Ringworld Engineers*  
- Anything with “how do we actually build this?” energy  

They love:
- Realistic construction  
- Tools, machines, and infrastructure  
- Problem‑solving under pressure  
- Characters who know what they’re doing  

Your novel’s detailed construction sequences, water tower engineering, bushcraft, and logistics are catnip for this group.
