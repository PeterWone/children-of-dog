# Recirculating Showers and Systems Thinking

## Concept: Efficient Water Use Through Design

### The System

**Recirculating shower with inline heating:**
- 20-30L initial fill
- Water sprays, collects in pan, filters (hair trap/screen), reheats, recirculates
- Gets progressively soapier (like bath water)
- Final fresh-water rinse (5-10L) when turbidity sensor triggers
- Total: 30-40L vs 150L traditional shower

**Key advantages:**
- 1/4 the water usage
- 1/4 the total energy (heating 25L once + maintenance vs constantly heating fresh cold water)
- Temperature stays perfectly consistent
- Actually MORE hygienic than a bath (flowing vs stagnant, final clean rinse)
- Long hot shower (20+ minutes) with minimal resource use

### Addressing the "Eww" Factor

**Cultural conditioning vs actual hygiene:**

A traditional bath:
- 80-150L clean water
- You sit in progressively dirtier water
- Soap, oils, skin cells accumulate
- Total immersion in contaminated water
- All water wasted afterward

Recirculating shower:
- Same principle (water gets progressively soapier)
- BUT: inline filtration removes debris continuously
- Water flows over you, doesn't pool
- Final fresh rinse leaves you cleaner than bath
- Uses 1/5 the water

**Japanese bathhouse culture already proves this works** - ritual washing before entering shared, recirculated bath water.

### Engineering Details

**Filter maintenance - the backflush solution:**

Problem: "Filter's gonna clog fast and be gross, soapy and full of hair like a drain."

Answer: "Nah, drains take years to get like that. We'll do cyclic backflush and dump so it never jams."

**How it works:**
- Timer triggers reverse flow every X minutes or after each use
- Backflush hits filter from clean side
- Debris dumps to grey water (vetiver-bound anyway)
- Filter stays continuously clean vs accumulating for months
- Same principle as self-cleaning pool filters

**Kent learns this the hard way:**
- Prototype ignores backflushing
- Filter clogs day three
- Hand-cleaning is gross
- Immediately implements automated backflush on v2

Lesson: **Active systems maintained properly don't get nasty. Passive systems ignored for months do.**

### Energy Efficiency

**Thermodynamics strongly favor recirculation:**

Traditional shower:
- Constantly heating fresh water (20°C → 40°C)
- 100L × 20°C delta = massive continuous draw
- 10 minutes might heat 150L total
- All heat energy down the drain

Recirculating:
- Heat 25L once (20°C → 40°C = 2,100 kJ)
- Water cools ~5°C per cycle (spray evaporation)
- Small heater maintains temp (5°C boost = ~525 kJ per cycle)
- 20-minute shower on 1/4 energy of 5-minute traditional

**Additional efficiencies:**
- Much smaller heater (500W vs 5kW instant)
- Perfect for solar thermal pre-heating
- Heat exchanger on drain for final rinse pre-heat
- Consistent temperature (no fluctuation)

## Thematic Elements

### Luxury Through Intelligence, Not Deprivation

Lumberjacket is a hedonist with systems literacy. He doesn't suffer deprivation.

**Perceived efficiency (city thinking):**
- High flow = efficient use of time
- Quick shower at 15L/min for 10 min = 150L
- Flush toilet 6-9L per flush
- Run tap while brushing/washing
- Result: 140L/day feels normal

**Real efficiency (systems thinking):**
- Navy shower = 20L total
- Composting toilet = ~0L
- Basin for teeth, water to plants
- Dish basin → vetiver
- Result: 50L/day feels LUXURIOUS with zero waste

Real luxury: 3-minute hot shower that gets you perfectly clean, not 20-minute shower where half the water never touches you. That's not sacrifice—that's **precision**.

A 20-minute hot shower using 35L and minimal energy isn't deprivation—it's **luxury through intelligence**.

## Water System Integration

### The Closed Loop

Fresh water → use → vetiver filtration → fruit tree irrigation → aquifer recharge → bore water

At 200 people, 50L/day = 10 tonnes/day, but most recirculates:
- Vetiver strips organics
- Trees take what they need
- Excess percolates to aquifer

Net loss is tiny (drinking water consumed, evaporation = ~10-15L/person actually leaves system permanently)

The rest is borrowed, cycling through productive uses.

### Grey Water Philosophy

Not waste—irrigation. Vetiver isn't ornamental—it's living filtration. Trees aren't decoration—they're water management.

Sustainable living isn't deprivation, it's intelligent design where water does multiple jobs before leaving the system.
