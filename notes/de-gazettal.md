# Getting rid of the public road

## STATE LAND DISPOSAL SEQUENCE FOR A DISCONTINUED ROAD RESERVE

1. **Road is discontinued**  
   → Road reserve ceases to be a public road.

2. **Land becomes unallocated state land**  
   → Managed by the state, not council.

3. **State must dispose of the land**  
   Options (in order of preference):  
   - Offer for sale to adjoining landholders  
   - Lease (rare for road reserves)  
   - Amalgamate into adjoining titles

4. **Offer for sale to adjoining landholders**  
   - Bisected landholders are offered their portion.  
   - If they decline, the state must choose another disposal method.

5. **Landholders decline purchase**  
   → Forces the state to move to amalgamation.

6. **State amalgamates land into adjoining titles**  
   - Standard outcome when land has no independent value.  
   - No cost to landholders.

7. **Access requirement check**  
   - If any landholder requires public access, state may object.  
   - If all landholders decline public access and accept easements, objection is removed.

8. **Final configuration**  
   - Neck of reserve purchased by frontage property.  
   - Remaining reserve amalgamated into bisected titles.  
   - Access provided by easement.  
   - Public access extinguished.

## Council Submission Cover Letter (incorporating the disposal logic)

This is the document you send to council.

**REQUEST FOR ROAD DISCONTINUANCE — BROWN GULLY ROAD**

To: [Council CEO / Infrastructure Director]  
From: [Your Name]  
Date: [Date]

I write on behalf of the landholders whose properties are bisected by the Brown Gully Road reserve.

We would like to assist Council to reduce unnecessary and ongoing expenditure and minimise foreseeable liability by initiating the formal process to discontinue (de‑gazette) Brown Gully Road on the following grounds:

1. **No public function**  
   The road is not a through road and provides no public services (power, water, waste, postal, school transport, or emergency dependency).

2. **Foreseeable safety hazard**  
   The road geometry (single lane, blind corners, soft shoulders) combined with periodic grading creates a foreseeable risk to unfamiliar drivers.

3. **Misalignment between legal status and physical reality**  
   The road functions as a private driveway for a small number of properties and has no broader transport role.

4. **Unnecessary public expenditure**  
   Ongoing maintenance provides no public benefit and exposes Council to avoidable liability.

5. **Landholder access preferences**  
   All affected landholders have signed declarations confirming:  
   - They do **not** require public road access;  
   - They prefer access via **easement**;  
   - They support discontinuance of the road reserve.

6. **State disposal pathway**  
   Following discontinuance, the state will offer the reserve for sale to adjoining landholders.  
   The bisected landholders will decline purchase, enabling the state to amalgamate the reserve into adjoining titles.  
   The frontage property will purchase the neck of the reserve to anchor easement access.

This pathway removes an ongoing cost and a foreseeable risk from Council’s responsibility while preserving legal access for all properties.

We request that Council commence the statutory process for road discontinuance and publish the required notice.

Sincerely,  
[Your Name]  
[Your Address]  
[Your Contact Details]
