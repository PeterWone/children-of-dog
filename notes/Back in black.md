# Back in black

## Time-line 

After Forest's death in arc4.

## Synopsis

Eric and the ground team are out attending to antithesis reports from farmers to the southwest of Tenterfield.

Increasing numbers suggest and their recon drones pinpoint a hive and they are fighting their way toward it when they realise there are _two_ hives and they have been manoeuvred into a killzone by seventeens. They dig in and get on the batphone to Sophia.

She can't just arrive and buy ammunition resupply in the field because they need the good stuff that Forest left them for situations like this.

Sophia and minions are thus frantically loading munitions into the Galya and then making a mercy dash, a few strafing runs to calm the dropzone and then unloading in a tearing hurry behind ground troops suppressive fire. 

Then Sophia in her black armour does her Vanguard thing, but it's not enough on its own they're holding while and Lulu and several of Bruce arrive. One Bruce on the back of the Triton, one on the Troopie and two behind on foot, Lulu driving the Troopie lifted up on cushions so she can see over the wheel.

## Character notes

**Sophia's emotional state:** Weary from complex moral problems (infrastructure destruction, sacrifice, Valkyrie burden), but **cheered greatly** by this simple problem with simple, violent solution. Clear tactical situation, obvious right action, no moral ambiguity. Relief through uncomplicated competence.

This is very Lulu thinking - Sophia has internalized some of her protégé's pragmatism. Not becoming Lulu, but appreciating the clarity of straightforward violence when it's warranted. Shows her Valkyrie transformation is complete - comfortable with necessary killing, especially when the choice is clean.

**Lulu's combat style:** Grinning ear to ear, leaping around pulverizing Antithesis **to the beat** of MC Hammer, singing along. For her, this is a big game - pure joy in competent violence. Her energy is infectious; the scene transforms from desperate last stand to "we're winning and this is absurd."

Ground team goes from existential terror to watching Lulu's dancing death performance, probably laughing despite themselves. The relief isn't just tactical (reinforcements arrived) but emotional (someone's having FUN and that means we're going to live).

**Bruce's loyalty clarified (future scene):**

Dusty at some point decides that since Bruce's interest and primary loyalty _clearly_ lies with Lulu, it's no longer reasonable to regard him as Dusty's.

Bruce's response: "Don't be absurd [that's my job!]. I cannot _imagine_ you making a casserole, much less Duck à l'Orange!"

"Bruce, you have _never_ made Duck à l'Orange."

"I _might_. The chances of it are much higher than _you_ making it."

**Dusty's counter-move (laconic, Forest-style manipulation):**

"Bruce. You're good at shooting things, I hear."

"Good, yes. I see your repertoire now extends to classic understatement, how splendid!"

"Those things [points up] are ducks. These [points to mesh bag] are oranges. This [significant pause] is your chance to impress Greaser, who tells me she never tried Duck à l'Orange."

**Translation:** Dusty is laconic and lazy like Forest, but far from stupid. He calls Bruce's bluff by providing actual ducks, actual oranges, and motivation (impressing Greaser). Minimal words, maximum manipulation. Forces Bruce to either make good on his hypothetical or admit it was just rhetoric. Classic valley competence - don't argue with words, create conditions that resolve the argument through action.

Bruce, of course, rises to the challenge. The Duck à l'Orange gets made. It's spectacular. Greaser is impressed. Dusty proves his point without ever explicitly stating it. Bruce proves his competence. Everyone wins. This is how valley problems get solved.

**The presentation is equally spectacular:**

Someone: "What _I_ want to know is where he got fine linen tablecloths, actual silverware - WTF even _is_ a tureen? - lead crystal and goose livers to make the pâté."

Sophia: "His bestie and champion source of ill-advised enthusiasm is Lulu. Lulu is a _Vanguard_. By now I expect she has some sort of specialised catering catalogue and the two of them spend hours ogling kitchen porn."

**Translation:** Lulu and Bruce's bonding activity is browsing high-end catering equipment and supplies. She buys him whatever he wants because she appreciates excellence and has infinite points. "What price a happy robot? It's _fun!_" Their relationship is built on shared enthusiasm for competence-through-style, whether that's theatrical combat or formal dining presentation. Bruce probably has better kitchen equipment than most restaurants. 

**The most expensive part:** Hammerspace fit-out so Bruce has everything on hand. This means he can produce elaborate formal dinners anywhere - field deployment, combat zone, wherever. Pull a copper reduction pan from hammerspace mid-battle if needed. The ultimate expression of "prepared for anything" - not just weapons and medical supplies, but also fine china, fresh ingredients, and cooking equipment. Very Bruce. Very Lulu to fund it without question.

**Scene example - Bruce deploys tablecloth:**

Bruce tutted disapprovingly at the bare trestle tables. Raising his arms like a conductor warning the pit, he flicked his shiny metal wrists and with a FWAP an enormous linen tablecloth settled into place.

"I see we're in good hands here. While your... friend works, let us repair to the maproom and debate the day."

"Dinner," Bruce announced in a severe tone, "will be at _precisely_ seven thirty. _Won't it, Dusty._ And as wine will be served, _no-one_ will bring half consumed beer bottles to the table. Will they."

**Translation:** Bruce's theatrical deployment of dining infrastructure is both impressive and intimidating. The disapproving tut + conductor gesture + perfect tablecloth deployment = clear message that standards will be maintained. Someone recognizes this is Bruce's domain now and diplomatically removes observers so he can work. Bruce then establishes the rules with pointed non-questions directed at Dusty (the likely offender). The severity, precision timing, wine service, and ban on half-consumed beer bottles = formal dining standards are NON-NEGOTIABLE. Dusty is being managed. The hammerspace makes impossible hospitality routine, but Bruce's personality makes it mandatory.

**The service staff:** One Bruce in Chef de Cuisine tocque, 37 Bruces in period maid outfits (serving). If someone somehow brings a beer bottle to table despite the warning, it would discreetly and swiftly disappear - perfect coordination through distributed consciousness means no lapses in service standards. The entire formal dining experience executed by one mind in 38 bodies. Perfect synchronization, no communication needed, absolute control of every detail. This is Bruce's ultimate expression - not just cooking well, but creating complete formal dining theater with himself as every role.

**"The Hammer" is Oleksiy's gravity hammer** from "Instant Replay" - neutronium head, inertial frame manipulation, only Lulu can lift it (Tictac hacked the grip). She already proved it against a hive (347 kills), bent the handle, Oleksiy gave it to her ("You keep. Suits you."). This isn't a new weapon - it's her signature tool, already established, already proven. When she says "I brought the Hammer" she means the physical weapon she's carrying, not ordering an orbital strike. This is personal, brutal, close-range violence with a weapon that has history.

**The Bruces are having a dance-off with the Antithesis** - syncopated Roman shield wall tactics (open, jab, close, advance a step) but theatrical and coordinated to the beat. Multiple bodies moving in perfect coordination, making lethal efficiency look like choreographed performance. Bruce flair = **Highlander garb (kilt, etc.)**, synchronized movement, making murder beautiful. The kilts swirl with each formation shift, adding visual drama to the tactical precision.

**One Bruce is the battle piper** - standing slightly back from the formation, playing bagpipes while the other three (or more) Bruces fight. Traditional Scottish Highland battle tradition enacted by distributed consciousness. The pipes either play alongside MC Hammer (bizarre mashup) or replace it with traditional Scottish war music (Scotland the Brave? Bonnie Dundee?). Either way, the sound is unmistakable and adds another layer of surreal to the combat.

**Ground team is BUSY** - still in active combat, suppressing fire, holding positions. Some stare in disbelief at the kilted Bruces doing synchronized combat dance before being snapped back to their own fight ("Eyes on your sector!"). They're not spectators - the absurdity is happening in their peripheral vision while they're trying not to die. Brief glimpses of synchronized Bruce movement and Lulu's grinning chaos between managing their own targets. The relief is real but the fight isn't over.

## Future Bruce Combat Themes

**Next appearance: Morris Dancers** - Traditional English folk dancers. Bells on legs, ribbons, sticks (which become weapons), handkerchiefs, the whole theatrical package. Synchronized stick-clicking becomes synchronized stick-fighting. The bells jingle with each coordinated strike. Even more absurd than Highland warriors, even more effective because Bruce commits completely to the bit.

**Other possibilities:**
- **Combat CanCan** - French cabaret dancers in frilly skirts. Synchronized high kicks that also kick Antithesis. Line formation, arms linked, kicking in perfect unison while maintaining lethal effectiveness. "Galop Infernal" playing. Feathered headdresses. The whole Moulin Rouge aesthetic weaponized.
- **Square Dancing** - Bruce square dances with HIMSELF as partners. Full caller routine ("allemande left," "dos-à-dos," "swing your partner"). One Bruce swings another Bruce around BY THE ARMS, using him as a weapon - Bruce's metal body becomes a flail. The swung Bruce kicks/strikes Antithesis while being spun. Perfect distributed consciousness combat: some bodies are weapons, some wield weapons, all coordinated through one mind. Caller Bruce stands to the side calling moves. Gingham and cowboy hats mandatory.
- **Cheerleader Pyramid** - Sixteen Bruces in full cheerleader outfits (pom-poms, pleated skirts, the works) form a human pyramid. Perfect stability through distributed consciousness - each body knows exactly how much weight/force to apply. Lulu climbs to the top, uses the elevated stable platform to fire missile/heavy weapon down hive entrance. Tactically sound (elevation, stability, clear line of sight) and aesthetically ridiculous (cheerleader outfits, pom-poms). Bruces possibly chanting/cheering while holding formation under fire. "GIVE ME AN A! GIVE ME AN N! GIVE ME A T! I! WHAT'S THAT SPELL? DEAD!"
- Welsh male voice choir (singing harmonies while fighting)
- Irish step dancers (Riverdance but violent, arms still at sides)
- Maypole dancers (ribbons become entanglement weapons)

**Pattern:** Bruce has no cultural boundaries when selecting theatrical combat themes. If it involves synchronized movement and traditional performance, he'll weaponize it. Each appearance is a surprise in style but not in effectiveness. The distributed consciousness allows perfect coordination regardless of how absurd the choreography. **The ultimate expression:** Using his own bodies as weapons against each other's momentum - only possible with distributed consciousness.

## Scene energy shift

**Before Lulu:** Desperate, dire, typing orbital strike codes, strategic sacrifice imminent

**After Lulu:** Still dangerous, still combat, but now they're COPING. Hammertime playing, Lulu dancing, Bruce(es) coordinating, ground team can breathe. Not safe, but survivable. 

The tonal whiplash is the point - from tragedy to absurdist action comedy without the threat actually diminishing. Lulu's joy doesn't make the Antithesis less dangerous, it just makes victory feel inevitable.
