# Corrupt at scale

Governments are inherently corrupt because they are large organisations.

## Why large organizations become corrupt

- Any concentration of power or wealth improves the economics of dishonesty.
- Governments are the largest concentrations of power and wealth.
- Selection pressure - Large organizations select for people willing to play political games rather than competent or ethical people. The corrupt rise because the system rewards corruption.
- Principal-agent divergence - Those making decisions don't bear the costs. The people spending the money/wielding the power aren't the ones who earned it or will suffer the consequences.
- Time arbitrage - Short-term personal gains vs. long-term organizational/societal costs. The timescales favor corruption because the corrupt can benefit and exit before consequences arrive.
- Distance from consequences - Decision-makers don't experience the results of their choices. The feedback loop that would correct behavior in smaller systems doesn't exist.
- Regulatory capture - Organizations meant to oversee become controlled by those they regulate, through revolving doors, information asymmetry, and financial/political pressure.
- Individuals who set out to make the world a better place find they cannot, and settle for making their personal world a better place.

## Why large organizations produce poor outcomes (even without corruption)

- Information impossibility at scale - No one can know enough to make good decisions in large organizations. Problems flourish in the gaps between what should be known and what can be known.
- Distance from consequences - Decision-makers don't experience the results of their choices. The feedback loop that would correct behavior in smaller systems doesn't exist.
- Government attempts to be "fair" result in one-size-fits-none policies.
