# Handling Things

> "The first rule of intelligence work is that nothing is as it seems."<br>
> &mdash; Anonymous

---

Leroy Chen had been handling field operatives for fifteen years. He knew the signs. The subtle shifts in tone, the creeping informality, the moment when "targets" became "people" in their reports. Usually you caught it early, rotated them out, let them decompress. Standard operational hygiene.

Margaret Torres's week six report sat on his screen, and he'd read it three times trying to decide if he was overreacting.

His colleague, David Brennan, stuck his head around the partition. "You still brooding over that valley assessment? It's been two hours."

"Come on, spill. What did she say?"

"Nothing and everything. It's disturbing."

"Quit playing coy, what did she say that's got you so worked up?"

Leroy took on a very odd expression and adopted a slightly theatrical British accent: "Day 63: The trees, so many trees. It is quiet here, and peaceful. The gift economy calls to me. Today I met an angel, but God called her and she had to go and teach children the Art of War. Sally's coffee is transcendent. Sometimes I think of home but the way is closed."

"She did _not_ write _that_!"

He stood and gestured at the chair. She sat and read for herself.

David's expression shifted from amused skepticism to professional alarm as he scrolled through Margaret's actual report. "Holy cow. She more or less did say that. Even if you _did_ paraphrase."

"So was this a profiling cockup at this end and she's a flake, or is there something funny going on in Hicksville?"

Leroy eyeballed him over his tortoiseshells.

"Don't look at me like that. I'm not going to bad-mouth her, they might send _me_ to live next to live antithesis. Anyway, you don't know she isn't a flake unless this isn't the first time."

A lizard-like slow blink and more eyeballing.

"She's not the first, is she."

Leroy pulled up a file directory. Margaret Torres was the second. Before her, Sophia Hachia, stopped filing reports two years ago and was now listed as "embedded - ongoing." 

"Four years."

"That's not a coincidence. That's a pattern."

"Well spotted. I can tell you work for Intel."

David read through more of Margaret's report, wincing at intervals. "So, um, why does she sound so happy to be swimming with the sharks?"

"Oh that, hang on a sec and I'll show you."

It took several minutes of rummaging through reports and their attachments. Eventually he produced a video clip. Margaret's face filled the screen, it was her one-on-one. A siren went off. She stood, and grabbed her phone, starting the camera. The video switched perspective and they were looking out the barred window from a second floor somewhere. Outside, what was recognisably a BVAC ground team but carrying kit that came from no terrestrial quartermaster set up defence in depth as though expecting attack along the dirt road, and then set off some sort of flare in their kill-box. And then the flood came.

"Was that a _lure_?"

"Yep."

"Where was the Vanguard?"

"She reports that it was a routine incident and they didn't notify him."

David stared at the frozen frame showing the aftermath. "Routine. They're hunting antithesis for sport and calling it routine."

"Not sport. It's 'Hot Training', according to her notes. They do it twice a month."

David shook his head, "I knew he was equipping them, it was in Hachia's reports. But _lures_? Those are expensive and dangerous, according to every Vanguard Biotech tried to acquire one from."

"Look at this." He pulled up a folder of photos. One showed Hachia in gleaming black power armour, like a cheerful gothic fantasy. She was beaming and toasting the camera operator with what looked like a cup of coffee.

David was quiet for a long moment. "She's not 'embedded', is she."

"Embedded is the official position."

"So we sent Margaret to say hi and keep her company. Because we're so caring and sharing. And her latest report basically says they don't care about government authority at all, they're spreading this model to neighbouring communities, and oh by the way one of our own people is now functionally a Vanguard herself."

"Uh huh."

"What does upstairs want us to do about it?"

Leroy removed his glasses, polished them carefully on his shirt. "That, David, is what we're about to go find out. Meeting in twenty minutes."

"Are they going to extract her?"

"Probably." He replaced his glasses, looked at Margaret's report one more time. "Whether she'll come willingly is another question entirely."

"And if she won't?"

Leroy closed the file. "Then we'll have lost our another operative. And we'll have to decide if we're prepared to send a third."

The question hung in the air between them: at what point did you stop calling it infiltration and start calling it surrender?

David headed for the door, then paused. "That bit about the angel teaching kids the Art of War. What was that about?"

"Did you look at Hachia's outfit?"

"Could hardly miss it. She looks like Black Widow."

David left, muttering something about needing stronger coffee.

Leroy sat alone with Margaret's report and a decision that was above his pay grade but would inevitably land back on his desk. You could extract an operative from a hostile environment. But what did you do when the environment was the opposite of hostile?

He started drafting his assessment for the meeting, knowing that every word would be scrutinized, that careers and budgets and possibly policy would hinge on how he framed this.

*The valley research project is a new kind of threat: a post-scarcity society with gift economics and technology unavailable to us. Recommend...*

"Fuck," he muttered, "I can't write that." He deleted the line and started again.

*Operative Torres shows signs of compromise consistent with previous assignments to this location. However, intelligence gathered remains valuable. Recommend...*

Delete.

The truth was simpler and more uncomfortable: they didn't have a protocol for this. You couldn't bomb people for being nice. You couldn't sanction a community for having good coffee and functional cooperation. You couldn't extract an operative who'd finally found somewhere she wanted to stay.

Leroy began typing his actual assessment, the one that would make his superiors deeply unhappy but had the virtue of being true.

*We're losing this engagement because we're fighting the wrong war.*
