# A Blast from the Past

> The cost of a thing is the amount of what I will call life which is required to be exchanged for it, immediately or in the long run.<br>
> &mdash; Henry David Thoreau, *Walden*

---

Archibald Grancock was not a happy camper. He was also very surprised when his call was answered.

"What's in the water down there, Sophia? You were strong willed. I half-way understood it when you decided to go your own way. But Margaret was not you. She wasn't self-made, _we_ made her. I know you don't personally owe me an explanation, but things have not gone unnoticed and questions are slowly becoming official."

The answer was faster than he expected.

"I can answer the question, Archie, but I don't know that you'll understand my answer. Did you read Margaret's reports? I thought she captured it very nicely."

"I read the summaries." 

Sophia switched on video. He followed suit, surprised by the gesture and not about to knock it back.

"Find the reports and read the sociological asides. You know how this place doesn't use money, right?"

"That's another matter, somewhat out of my purview. But I don't think I'd like to be in your shoes when the tax department comes knocking. And it will. They'll estimate the value of goods and services changing hands at current market rates and then they deem your incomes. If your own reports are to be believed fifty families have acquired houses on acreage built from solid hardwood. One of them is practically a castle. Not even the PM has a castle."

"The price of a house here is friendship and presence." She sighed. "Archie, I knew you wouldn't understand. It's not your fault, this doesn't make sense within your frame of reference. What do they tell us, Archie? Conform, do as you're told, work hard and you'll get money. But what's money _for_? Food, water, shelter, medicine? We have all that. Food grows on trees. Water falls out of the sky. The houses you say are so valuable, we made ourselves. The kids are incredibly healthy, because it's clean out here and they get a lot of exercise. Then there is the matter of trust. You're a well educated fellow, Archie. I imagine you know what Dunbar's number is."

"Yes."

"This community is two-hundred and twenty people. Dunbar's number is about two-thirds of that. What that means is that any given person is personally known to two thirds of the people present and the other third know someone who knows him. There are no strangers, Archie. So there's trust."

"What does trust have to do with anything?"

"I knew you wouldn't understand. Let's try again. Alright. Having a bit of money is better than not having money because why?"

"What?"

"It's not a trick question. These people have a profoundly different way of looking at the world. It took me two years and a personal crisis to really understand. I'm fast-tracking you by feeding you the right questions. The answers are for you, not me."

"Alright, I'll play your little game. Having 'a bit' of money gives you food, shelter, transport, everything you need to go earn some more. Not having it means you're homeless and hungry. What about it?"

"Why is having significant money better than having a bit of money?"

"Better stuff, more stuff, time off to do the things you like to do."

"And pots of money?"

"Do what you like doing all the time."

"These people do what they like doing, all the time. All of them."

There was a stunned silence while his mind struggled with the idea that a bunch of rebels out in the sticks had the privileges of pots of money.

"They _bribed_ you?"

She burbled with laughter at this unexpected silliness.

"No. They invited me to dinner. For that matter he asked _me_ for a favour."

"A Vanguard wanted a favour? Why is this the first time I'm hearing about it? You cannot tell me you didn't know what that's _worth?_"

"He bought the title for the land. He wanted the deed. The state wouldn't hand it over. He said that if I could help, he would count it a great favour. I checked the law, found that _centuries_ of precedent supported his position, and took steps to make good. Out of pocket, I might add. But we're well off topic. Ask yourself where the castle came from, Archie. I know you spy on us, I used to _do_ it for you. Did you see money change hands? Were materials brought in?"

"You know there weren't, or at least we can't find it."

"So where did the castle come from?"

"Jesus Sophie, _I_ don't know. I sent you, and then Margaret, to find out this stuff. All I got were embarrassingly bland resignations."

"That's not entirely true. I filed a lot of reports before my point of view changed." She tilted her head in thought. "You ever watch an Amish barn-raising on youtube, Archie?"

"Yes?"

"We've had a few of those. We also had a community castle-raising. It ran for about three weeks, and it was a lot of fun. We were all-in on that one. Ken's very popular with the ground-team lads."

"What ground team? They all resigned! It was even harder to hide than _your_ resignation."

"We still have a ground team. We still have both teams. You do know we live right next to a bunch of antithesis, right?"

He rummaged through a pile of dossiers on his desk. Halfway through one his face filled with a mix of alarm and astonishment.

"Ken the PTSD guy is _popular?_ We sent him there for suicide by antithesis, he was a total liability."

"Yeah, he really found his feet here. Found something to live for. Built a home. Goes fishing with his boy. They make pickles together. Forest showed 'em how. He's a pillar of the community. Knows exactly what combat stress looks like and gets to 'em before they start to flake, knows exactly what to say because he's lived it."

"What do you want, Sophie? You can't build a private army and expect the state to leave it alone."

"It's not an army. It's not even a militia. It's a pair of ground teams that help a regional Vanguard manage antithesis incursions."

"Associating with a Vanguard does not make it better. You know as well as I do they are vigilantes with no regard for procedure or propriety. We only tolerate them because..." his voice trailed off.

"Because they do necessary work, you don't have to pay them and it's easy to blame all the world's problems on them?" Video was still on. He could see the half smile flicker across her face. "It's certainly one interpretation of the facts. In some cases it might even be true."

"Your change of heart does nothing to excuse the fact that your little band of merry men represent a snub to the authority of the state, and that _cannot_ be ignored."

"Why not? You ignore it for 'vigilantes with no regard for propriety' and the world hasn't ended."

"Individuals who are already discredited. You've built a _community_ that ignores the state. It's a direct challenge to authority."

"Mmm. A quiet little micro-nation that doesn't cause trouble, but is quite good at dealing with trouble when it arrives."

"Are you back-chatting me?"

"No, Archie. I don't work for you any more, remember? Go back and re-read _my_ reports. All you have to do is not poke the bear."

"It's not _me_ that's poking a bear!"

"We're not interested in anything outside this valley."

"And yet every time there's an incursion you ride to the rescue, doing good works the length and breadth of the Granite Belt and incidentally recalibrating regional perception of Vanguards as selfish property wreckers. For people who aren't interested in anything outside your valley you've been running an incredibly effective hearts and minds campaign."

"We can't leave them to die, Archie. If we did we'd be the monsters you paint us to be. We treat _everyone_ decently. To answer your original question, she left because she found a home. She's surrounded by people she trusts who don't want anything from her but her happiness and friendship."

"It's a goddamn cult."

"Is that an official position? I can use that when the tax department calls."

He hung up, and she sighed again. It had been inevitable.

Maybe they _were_ a cult. They had their own received wisdom: rule none and suffer none to rule. And the way the kids related to Autumn she might have been a nature goddess. Or a disembodied big sister. Was there a difference?

She thought about the kid they rescued in Glen Innes, and wondered how she was getting along.
