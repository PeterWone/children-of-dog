Sophia, Bruce (silent, in the Victorian dress), and Lulu walk up to Parliament House. The ceremonial guards are polite. She is polite back: they'll be going inside, she'd prefer not to hurt anyone, if they shoot she'll take the weapon and let them go. One gets a shot off. Bruce catches the round and punts it back into the man's vest. She bends the rifle barrel by hand. They go inside.

In the chamber she opens with a Socratic question: what do the Roman Empire, Parliament, and every other empire have in common? Autumn brings Forest's face to life — large as life, you could almost smell oil and woodchips — and he gives the answer himself. They centralised. Centralisation is the crystal meth of civilisations. They get stronger, faster, smarter — and then they can't perform without it, and then a whole people dies. Sophia lays out the sovereignty argument with precision: the constitution protects the union of states, not individuals; property exceptions are explicit; land is not among them; retrospective law is beneath contempt. The Speaker challenges her right to speak at all. She rises into the air. "We are not terrorists. We have done nothing but rule ourselves. Why would you find that terrifying?" The power goes out on cue. In the darkness she addresses the tactical squad directly, thanks them for their service, leaves them solvent for their gear and a personal apology. She drops a precision strike on the road outside the army stores as she leaves — road empty, nobody hurt, leaflets fluttering from a conspicuously empty sky. Last warning. Stop or be stopped. SH.

She sits on the edge of the roof afterward, weeping. Lulu sits with her. She is not weeping for the theatre of it — she is weeping because she can see what comes next and she has no option. They can't help themselves, and because they can't she will have no choice but to make their prison their tomb.

Lulu tells her she looks like Batman. Sophia objects: Black Widow, at most. Lulu holds up a phone showing Natasha Romanov in white armour. Autumn comments — and names her. Forest left Lulu a Vanguard name: Hervor. A small woman who defied fate, confronted her dead father, demanded her cursed weapon, and had in her the courage and the strength to break empires. He hardly knew her, Lulu says. "He watches all who walk between worlds."

Then the other names fall: Autumn names Sophia Skuld, and Lulu threatens to call her Dögun for the rest of her life if Sophia ever uses Hervor in public. Fade to black. The last line: Bruce read her the sagas at bedtime. She wanted to be a Valkyrie. She was too small. That rotten robot ratted her out to the old man.

This is the arc's closing movement and the book's thesis made explicit. The speech in Parliament is not the point — the weeping on the roof is. Sophia is not triumphant. She is a person who can see exactly how this ends and is doing it anyway, because no one else can. The Valkyrie does not carry the living from the field. She carries the chosen. She already knows the cost.

The naming completes the pantheon's assembly. Odin named them both from beyond the pyre, through Autumn, who has been his instrument throughout. Hervor: the cursed weapon reclaimed, the berserker who breaks empires. Skuld: one of the three Norns, the one whose name means "that which shall be" — fate, obligation, what must come to pass. The arc is called Apotheosis. The fade to black lands on the correct word.
