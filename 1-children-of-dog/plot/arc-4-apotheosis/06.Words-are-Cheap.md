Sophia has no points to spend and no catalogues to raid. What she has is time, good people, and a Class VII AI who can turn Forest's notebooks into content people will actually watch. The plan is simple because it has to be: a documentary series called the Treadmill — eight episodes, one a week, each a clean lesson in how centralisation leads to collapse, using Egypt, Rome, China, Europe, America, and the present as cases. No moralising, no villains. Just the pattern.

Distribution is deliberately uncontrolled: free content at high quality, given to networks and town libraries and schools and pubs with a decent wall. Autumn handles signal hygiene. Sophia writes her intros in plain Australian — she wants mums, sparkies, nurses, farmers, and council workers to nod along. She calls it inoculation. She knows it is propaganda. It is also testimony. "Roll the first lesson." Autumn knows it probably won't work. But perhaps the horse will sing.
