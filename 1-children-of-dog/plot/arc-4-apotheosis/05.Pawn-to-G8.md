Sophia reviews Forest's notebooks with Autumn. The plan is designed around her constrained starting position — no catalogues, almost no points — because he knew exactly what she would inherit. Most of it is Class Zero. The ground teams are well-kitted and know how to play the game. The bees are the one real problem: half the fleet has been stood down to guarantee six months of attrition, and those remaining are in low-power mode. "Not a leaf shall fall" is no longer operable. Sophia's response is immediate — tell the kids the truth, recruit them to be Autumn's eyes and ears. She sees in five seconds what Autumn hadn't: the diminishment will strengthen the dynamic rather than break it.

She asks about Lulu, and the story comes out properly for the first time in the narrative: Bruce found a seven-year-old alone in a collapsed building in Glen Innes, a kitchen knife in her hand, making dogweeds pay for every centimetre. He put her on his shoulders, handed her a spotter role, and let her pull the trigger rather than removing her from danger. When the shelters opened, her mother found her on top of a two-metre battlebot in bandoliers, grinning. "No, mum. Can't you see I'm working?" Autumn tells Sophia she is twelve now, and a Vanguard. Sophia laughs hysterically. Of course she is. She invites her to the valley.

Lulu arrives in a bad temper — her parents watched her get shot on national television, and they keep calling her Chloe. "Chloe is dead," she tells them. "She died under the kitchen table in Bowden Street while you were safe inside a shelter." They are apparently more disturbed that her seat of consciousness is no longer in her head than by the bullet itself. She gives up and comes to the valley instead.

She and Sophia set up hay bales and some of the valley kids teach them archery. Sally opens the café. Nobody asks Lulu about her parents or her head. Nobody asks Sophia about Forest or what comes next. The kids lecture them on anchor points and repeatability — aim at the same point until your groups are tight, then fiddle with the sight — and Lulu, bow tilted to compensate for its length, punches steadily tightening groups into the bale, losing arrows in the undergrowth because the bow is overpowered for hay.

The chapter's title is a chess term: a pawn that reaches the eighth rank is promoted to any piece the player chooses, almost always a queen. The pawn who becomes a Valkyrie. The chapter is the first moment of Sophia's new life after the inheritance — not grief, not planning, but sitting in the afternoon sun with a borrowed bow and a twelve-year-old Vanguard, being taught by children. The closing image is the sun heading for the horizon: slow, but steady, and it gets there. She will too.
