Sophia is testing a plan. She instructs Autumn to fire at the first available eight-second window on a specific road, landing at the two-thirds mark between passing vehicles so the approaching car can stop. She wants a proven four-second safe window, not a calculated one. "I don't do last stands and I don't use untested plans. I don't want to think — I want to know." Autumn finds this unnecessary and says so. Sophia's answer: "And shortly there will be two of us."

The next morning, talkback radio is jammed. Blue-white streak across the sky, felt in chests, windows rattled, dogs going spare, a hole in the road, cranky blokes with brooms. The Desert Fireball Network caught two faint streaks, not enough for a full solve. Weather radar showed a transient. A pop-science magazine runs a careful post-mortem by lunchtime: small natural bolide, conservative call, nothing to worry about, sleep easy. Nobody noticed that the timing and trajectory were uncannily perfect for the road to be empty. Sophia reads the piece with tired eyes and closes the tab. "Satisfied?" Autumn asks. Sophia says nothing. She'd already had the last word.

The test implies a planned precision strike on a target where the margin for civilian casualties is zero and there will be no second chance. The four-second window is not curiosity — it is a specification. She is not testing whether the weapon works. She is testing whether it can be placed with surgical accuracy in a live environment, disguised as a natural event, without anyone noticing the difference. The scientific establishment spent a full news cycle explaining why they should not be alarmed, and the one thing that would have alarmed them — the uncanny perfection of the timing — went unnoticed by everyone except the person who arranged it.

This is what the Valkyrie armed for deliberate war looks like. Not the reactive defence of chapter 19, but the cold pre-mission checklist of someone who has identified a target, designed a plan, and will not execute it until she has personally verified every variable. Forest trusted Autumn implicitly and improvised brilliantly. Sophia trusts Autumn completely and tests anyway. The catsuit is not the lumber jacket, and she knows the difference.
