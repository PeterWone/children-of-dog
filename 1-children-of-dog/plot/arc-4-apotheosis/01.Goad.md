Movie night in the valley — the Avengers on a sheet pinned to the workshop wall. On screen, Fury throws the dead agent's trading cards on the table: "They needed a push." Forest watches Sophia's profile in the projector light and murmurs to himself: "A little push. Something to believe in." He taps Oleksiy's mug with his own and they drink without speaking. The storm is coming. He knows it. He goes to meet it.

What follows is a sustained, deliberate, magnificently petty campaign to force the government's hand. Forest shows up at incursions in his red check jacket with news helicopters circling, theatrical and efficient. He shoots Channel 7's tail rotor in front of the ABC camera, then pulls both crew clear and gives a double thumbs-up while walking away. He does not touch the journalist. Graffiti drones paint corporate crimes across CBD towers in letters taller than a man. **DID YOU SIGN A SOCIAL CONTRACT?** goes on law schools and the High Court. **WHEN LAW IS UNPOPULAR WHO IS REPRESENTED?** crosses Parliament House. Treasury gets the critique in the Treasury's own economic language, making the Secretary's rebuttal look foolish. **ROMANES AEUNT DOMUS!** hits fifty government buildings in a hundred styles. The Sydney Harbour Bridge gets **LAND RIGHTS FOR GAY WHALES! YOUR TAXES AT WORK** — progressive enough to confuse the left, absurd enough to enrage the right, impossible to ignore. He calls in live to a current affairs panel to observe pleasantly that if the law applied to him someone would have applied it by now. The government goes quiet for three days. He starts targeting individual politicians' fences with public-record figures: property values, investment portfolios, net worth increases since taking office. They stop talking to him directly and start talking about him in closed sessions instead.

None of it is random. Forest is a conductor and the government is his orchestra. He is goading them into making a move — a specific move, at a specific time — because he needs Sophia to have something to believe in when it happens, and the only thing that will produce it is the state finally overreaching against a man who is visibly, undeniably saving lives. Autumn confirms they are building a legal case for extraordinary measures. He asks "How long?" and means: is there enough time?

The final scene is Sophia, alone in the darkened longhouse after the project has wound down, tracing her finger on the oiled timber of the long table. She pulls out a bundle of rags and a bottle of oil and begins polishing — wax on, wax off — travelling through time. She thinks it might be a bannister. Autumn projects the image she first showed her when she was planning her house: aged timber, dark and lustrous, burnished by countless hands. Then Autumn plays Stevie Nicks: *and if you build your house, then call me home.* "You never did," Autumn says. "Maybe we should."

The chapter is arc 4's opening statement. Arc 3 ended with Sophia planning a home; arc 4 begins with that thread pulled taut. Forest is engineering a crisis not because he wants to destroy the old world but because without one, the people he loves will never fully commit to building the new one. The goad is for them, not for the government.
