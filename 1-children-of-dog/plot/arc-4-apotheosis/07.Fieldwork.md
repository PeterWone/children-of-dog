For three years, Sophia's belter teams move through the outlying regions: evacuations ahead of antithesis sweeps, bridge repairs, field clinics, well-digging. Forest called it fieldwork. She calls it the same thing now. The work is genuine — the need is real, the suffering is real, the help is real — and it is also deliberate. Every person sheltered, every system restored is evidence. Proof that another way is possible. Demonstration of competence and care spreading through community networks faster than any announcement.

Three water reticulation systems restored. A coast road bridge repaired that had bled local economies for years. A medical circuit running twice a year through towns government healthcare abandoned. The belters are not popular because they announced themselves. They are trusted because they showed up when nobody else did.

Sophia is troubled by the intentionality underneath the kindness. Autumn: "Propaganda works because it has a seed of truth. This isn't a seed of truth. This is truth all the way down."
