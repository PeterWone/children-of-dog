Before dawn, Sophia sits at the fabrication station watching drones take shape — Autumn's latest refinement, better than she can afford, better than she has earned. She feels like a spoilt heiress playing with a dead man's tools. Autumn explains patiently: Forest spent it as he went. Gave it away. Pooled it. Invested it in fabricators and blueprints that anyone could use. What she is calling his legacy was always meant to outlive him.

The harder conversation: what does she owe him? Autumn gives two answers. She owes him nothing — he made her into a weapon and that was a gift freely given. She owes him everything — be the weapon. The two answers are not contradictory. Somewhere in the middle is the thing Maria explained: "You will serve, Valkyrie." Sophia stares at the fabricator, looks pleased against her better judgment, and tells Autumn not to tell anyone.
