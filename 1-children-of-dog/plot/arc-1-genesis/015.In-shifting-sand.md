# In Shifting Sand

A series of vignettes from Forest's youth, showing how he came to understand systems, rules, and the gaps between them. He brews accidentally-alcoholic ginger beer for the town fishermen, gets caught by a police officer who exercises quiet discretion. He and a schoolmate invoke an 1847 pastoral law to demand hay and water for their horses outside a police station, wrong-footing a hostile constable until the sergeant sees the joke and plays along. In class, he and his friends see through the superannuation scheme as a confidence trick before the ink is dry.

Each story is a lesson learned — but not the polite version. Laws are written by the powerful to serve themselves; the gaps protect insiders. Authority constantly exercises discretion while claiming it has none — a lie that insulates enforcers from accountability. The letter of the law is a weapon that cuts both ways, and those who wrote it never expected you to read it carefully. Institutions that claim to protect you are primarily designed to extract from you. These are the formative experiences behind the man who will one day take down a government.
