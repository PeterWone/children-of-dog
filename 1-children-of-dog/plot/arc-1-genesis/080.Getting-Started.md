Forest and Autumn establish their working relationship on awkward terms. He refuses to engage with the Vanguard point-accounting system, dismissing it as bean-counting. He rejects the default interface name and calls his AI "Autumn", ostensibly after Vivaldi's Four Seasons. She humours this and pretends she doesnt know it's a reference to a warship in a video game.

Without warning, a government and corporate delegation arrives at the valley. The group includes CSIRO researchers, Project Liaison Sophia Hachia — platinum-haired, icy blue-eyed, and visibly frustrated by the Vanguard programme's structural immunity to bureaucratic leverage — along with corporate representatives from Austral Defense Solutions and the Advanced Materials Consortium. Autumn has already moved behind the scenes, arranging a research subsidy built around Australia's unique antithesis-resistant ecology. The delegation is here to formalise what she has already made inevitable.

Forest plays along, agreeing to host the researchers he dubs "interns." Dr Marcus Kent bristles at the title. Sophia quickly realises she has no standard tools to use against a Vanguard operating in good faith on his own land; everything she reaches for is already out of her grasp. The chapter establishes the central tension of the arc: a competent bureaucrat meeting a system that cannot be bureaucratically managed.
