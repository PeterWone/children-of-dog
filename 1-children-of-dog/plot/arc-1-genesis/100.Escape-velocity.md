Forest reviews the aftermath of the incursion and finds himself quietly resentful: his private valley is becoming a science project. A formal delegation of researchers and corporate representatives arrives, and Autumn has already arranged a research subsidy anchored in Australia's unique ecology — the valley is now officially a field station whether Forest likes it or not.

He plays along, agreeing to host the researchers as "interns," a framing the academics find undignified. Sophia Hachia, the Project Liaison, quickly discovers that none of her usual bureaucratic levers work on a Vanguard operating on private freehold land. She has authority in every system except the one that matters here. Forest has reached escape velocity from the institutional world, and everyone in the room knows it.
