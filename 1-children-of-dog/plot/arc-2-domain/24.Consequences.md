Forest thinks through the geopolitical implications of what he is doing. The valley cannot hide the fall of a nation; when central authority fails at sufficient scale, everyone will know. His plan is to play the major powers off against each other — invite American corporations into the vacuum while the dust is settling, but require them to honour existing Chinese contracts. This is not naivety; it is a calculated use of mutual economic interest to prevent a new scramble.

Autumn is theatrically horrified by the cold-bloodedness of the thinking. Then she agrees it is the right answer. This alignment is, in its way, more unsettling than the plan itself.
