Janna leads a dawn procession through the valley: two hundred birds and a scattering of children, the whole column moving to tend the dams and perform ecological management tasks that would otherwise require machinery and chemicals. The birds have distinct operational roles — geese lead and set pace, ducks investigate water margins, chickens manage the middle ground, guinea fowl provide security — and they perform them with the easy competence of animals doing what they were bred to do.

A child rides a goat. The whole thing is a fairy tale made literal. The chapter is pure pleasure.
