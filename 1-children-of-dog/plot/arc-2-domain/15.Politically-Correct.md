Rebecca Morrison, a researcher's wife, cannot reconcile what she observes in the valley with the collectivist ideologies her political frame requires her to fear. She brings it to the kitchen table, and a discussion breaks out among the valley women. They explain: this is not collectivisation. There is no distribution committee, no bureaucracy, no enforcement mechanism. It is neighbours helping neighbours, which is something entirely different.

The gift economy survives not because it is enforced but because the valley has higher technology than the outside world — it cannot be outcompeted on its home ground. Rebecca does not fully accept this, but she stops arguing. The chapter is a comedy of category error: a woman trying to fit something genuinely new into her existing mental map and finding it won't go.
