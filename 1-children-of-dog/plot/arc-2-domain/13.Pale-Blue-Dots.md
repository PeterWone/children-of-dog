Forest pauses his brush-clearing and asks Autumn what other inhabited worlds look like. Her answer is a full lesson in planetary chemistry. The worlds she shows him are recognisable — green plants, blue skies, liquid oceans — and he assumes panspermia. She tells him he is asking the wrong question.

The right question is not why the worlds look similar, but why they would look different. Life emerges under a narrow range of conditions, and the universe is large enough that even narrow ranges produce millions of candidates. What he is seeing is not convergence — it is the only solution to the equation. They work through photosynthesis, chlorophyll, carbon chemistry, and the constraints that make biology essentially the same everywhere. The conversation ends with Forest marvelling at the idea that he is what carbon atoms do when they get clever. Autumn's final joke at his expense lands perfectly.
