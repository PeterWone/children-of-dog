Forest researches how screws are mass-produced — cold heading, thread rolling, the industrial logic that makes a ten-cent fastener possible. He reflects on the valley's cottage-industry and high-science hybrid: people are building stone cottages, making bricks, throwing pottery, while also working with Class I Vanguard technology.

He nurses a fantasy of a network of specialised villages, each feeding itself producing a specialty thing and sharing the surplus with other villages. He notes the irony that the valley's motto is "do it right" and that they will probably end up doing everything again anyway, better. The chapter is a quiet meditation on craftsmanship and the pleasure of understanding how things work.
