Forest visits a classroom and asks the students to name a civilisation that collapsed for reasons that don't ultimately come down to centralisation. None of them can. He teaches about monoculture and systems fragility, using Janna's diversified garden as a model and imperial grain agriculture as its opposite. The students engage seriously; Forest uses Socratic method throughout, asking rather than telling.

The chapter works as both a lesson in history and a lesson in how Forest teaches — by making his students do the thinking, so the conclusions feel discovered rather than received. They will remember this.
