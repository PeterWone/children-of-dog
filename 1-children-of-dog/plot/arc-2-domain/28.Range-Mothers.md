The range mothers watch weapons training from a pavilion, wrapped in earmuffs, comfortable with thermoses and each other. The girls — Lisa (a valley child) and Sophia Hachia — catch the boys who haven't cleaned their rifles and enforce compliance with the easy authority of people who have been paying attention longer than anyone else.

Oleksiy and Eric give an impromptu maintenance demonstration of Old Faithful, Oleksiy's own weapon, lovingly described. Eric's dry humour runs throughout. The scene makes plain that weapons maintenance has become part of the valley's social fabric — routine, competent, observed by mothers who have made their peace with the fact that this is how their children grow up here, and who have decided it is good.
