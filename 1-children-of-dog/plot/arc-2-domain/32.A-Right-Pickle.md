Forest tells Scott the story behind the Banana Shire — named, as it turns out, for a bull's anatomy rather than the fruit. Scott has been fishing. Forest reflects on the ecology of the local waterways: how catching the big Murray cod disrupted the predator balance, causing spangled perch to breed like mosquitoes, which then became excellent cray bait. He traces the food chain with the easy affection of a man who finds this kind of thing genuinely delightful.

The conversation winds back to fishing with his father. It is companionable and digressive, and it sets up the aquaculture context: these waterways have always been managed, deliberately or accidentally, by the people who live beside them.
