Eloise — mechanical engineer, factory automation background, real degree — is reading the reboot guides and feeling overwhelmed. The depth of industrial knowledge required is staggering: every system depends on prior systems that must themselves be built. She is not failing; she is trying to compress civilisation into a single head, and the weight of it is showing.

Autumn reassures her plainly and without condescension. The range mothers drift in to listen — Rebecca, Sarah, Monica — drawn by the sound of someone speaking honestly about an impossible task. Autumn addresses Eloise's frustration directly: tools to make tools to make tools is the real problem, and it has a real answer, and the answer is time and community and starting where you are. The fabber debate sprawls across days — is using a machine you don't fully understand the same as using motors you didn't wind yourself? The argument goes in circles until Autumn frames it: the question isn't whether you made it, it's whether your community understands it well enough to use it wisely and teach it to the next generation. Eventually Eloise argues for the fabber with engineer's pragmatism, Forest is revealed to have known about Dusty's fabber all along and was waiting to see what she'd work out on her own, and Autumn is quietly delighted.

Then Eloise asks about augmentations. She has the body language of someone asking for a date. Autumn projects herself as a devil on Eloise's shoulder, voluptuous and available, and her voice carries something new — sultry, the voice of temptation — while warning honestly that for people like them, this kind of thing can be intoxicating and addictive, and that depending on how far it goes there is a real chance of losing yourself. Eloise, gazing into an impossible future bearing down on her like a freight train on the rails of her own desire, asks in a small voice: "But you'd be there, right?" The smile over the presence drone has more than warmth in it. Forest notices Autumn's faraway look and asks how her little protégé is getting on — and whether she has worked out how she plans to consummate. Autumn gives him stink-eye, which only encourages him.
