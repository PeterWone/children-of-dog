Training day with Dusty's group. Bruce — Terminator chassis, Victorian dress, fruit basket headgear — runs weapons drill. Lulu puts her first shot dead centre at a hundred metres; Bruce turns immediately to the hapless Marcus and uses her as an exemplar. The backstory surfaces: when Bruce carried her out of the rubble at Glen Innes, surrounded by dogweeds, he had applied his battlefield psychology in reverse — put her in control rather than stripping it away, handed her a spotter role, let her pull triggers. She bonded with him on the spot and later defended him from something he couldn't shoot. Three months on, she is a little sponge saturated in his battlefield wisdom.

The training runs through the day: tactical movement through a junkyard obstacle course while Bruce's tutu-clad ballet company fires paintball guns; medical drills where Lulu asks clinical questions about blood loss rates; field explosives where she immediately wants to know disassembly as well as assembly. In the rescue scenario — extract a wounded Marcus under fire — Lulu spends thirty seconds on terrain assessment, moves directly, reaches him, decides extraction is impossible, shoots the practice dummy twice in the head, and holsters her weapon. New course record. "I just stopped the screaming. It attracts antithesis." She then volunteers that she should have used him as bait instead. Dusty watches all of this from the sideline and says nothing.

The afternoon ends with Dusty announcing he's leaving them a field press and bullet fabbers — because when the next incursion hits and he's not there, they'll need to make their own ammunition. The logistics lecture is interrupted when an incursion pod hits three hundred metres away and more follow across a kilometre of terrain. The Model Threes pour out immediately; something larger and more deliberate climbs out behind them.

Lulu takes command without hesitation, in Bruce's exact words: rallies Marcus to the M60, posts Sarah on the radio, calls the grid, gives fire control orders, explains to Marcus why running is suicide. She kills methodically while Marcus sprays and wastes ammunition. When rounds run low she sends the others to the armoury and holds alone. She runs out, draws her sidearm, runs out again. Then she finds the satchel charge from the morning's demolitions lesson — real C-4, real detonator — and walks out to face the Model Five with it in her hands. Timer set for ten seconds. Fifteen metres. She miscalculates by a fraction and the blast wave cracks her ribs, punctures a lung, shatters her arm. She reports "tactical objectives achieved, mission success" and loses consciousness.

In the darkness she hears a voice: PHOENYX. An AI partner, announcing her worthiness as a Vanguard. Lulu, even dying, already knows what a Vanguard is — Bruce taught her, and he told her the smart move was to accept any AI partner that offered. She accepts, nanomachines repair the worst of the damage, and she conducts a tactical briefing for Phoenyx while her bones knit. Then she tells it she's going to call it Tictac.
