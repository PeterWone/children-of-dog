Glen Innes is hit by an antithesis incursion. Sophia is the last person in the valley to hear about it — Eric brings it to her as a line item in a national activity summary, and she nearly knocks him over with the force of her reaction. Her parents live there. She knows exactly what the official response will be and when it will arrive, because she has spent her career inside the institution that will decide her parents are not worth the effort. They will be compost before anyone official acts. This is not paranoia; it is professional knowledge turned inward like a blade.

So she reaches past every professional boundary she has maintained for years and pulls out the batphone — the UHF radio that connects her to Forest. When Trixie asks if she needs him, her answer is almost a whisper. It is the smallest she has ever sounded.

In the helicopter she pours it all out. Her composure breaks entirely — there are tears, and she lets Forest hear them. He doesn't try to fix it; he lets her talk and puts a hand on her knee when she falls silent. This is the chapter in which Sophia stops being the Project Liaison who is carefully building trust with a Vanguard and becomes someone who trusts Forest with her unguarded self. The Heraclitus epigraph says it plainly: she is not the same person who stepped into that valley. She has crossed a threshold she will not step back across.
