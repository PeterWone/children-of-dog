Margaret's final report, written at the town markets. Janna is selling unusual vegetables — skirret, scorzonera, orach — and Margaret asks why she stocks things nobody has heard of. Janna explains she is not there to make money; the till is a stage prop. She is there to educate and to get unusual plants into people's gardens.

Valley children explain the rest to Margaret in the direct way of children who have genuinely absorbed something: "Hearts and minds, and decoupling." White-anting the commercial food system through kindness — handing out samples, teaching people to grow things, making the community's food supply incrementally independent. Margaret understands. She has been watching economic subversion through hospitality for four months, and she only now sees it whole.
