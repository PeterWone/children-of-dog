Sophia is learning guitar. A string snaps and Forest appears in the doorway with a toolkit and the easy manner of someone who has fixed broken instruments his entire life. He re-strings it and teaches her the mechanics. From there the conversation opens into the history of tuning systems — Pythagorean, meantone, well temperament, equal temperament — and Autumn plays examples of each. Equal temperament is technically versatile and spiritually flat; it is the compatibility patch that made the Stockwell of the world possible.

Later the amp crackles, and Forest produces a soldering iron. When he is done he plays a melody — B7, A7, E or E7, a blues progression he has been carrying around for years. It does not have a name. Sophia watches his hands and asks what it is called. He shakes his head. The chapter is a quiet afternoon between people becoming friends.
